How we compare them

Is this a fair comparison?

As fair as the published figures allow. For both hospitals we use the main, consultant-led emergency department only (Wales calls it a major emergency department, England a Type 1 A&E), not minor injury units. Both count the time from arrival to being admitted, transferred or discharged.

Where do the 12-hour figures come from?

For Wrexham Maelor, from the Welsh Government's monthly emergency department figures. For the Countess, from NHS England's Emergency Care Data Set analysis, which NHS England publishes as experimental, rounds to the nearest five patients, and first issues as provisional before confirming it a month later. NHS England's headline 12-hour figure measures something different (the wait after a decision to admit), so we do not use it here.

When do the figures update?

England publishes on the second Thursday of each month and Wales usually later in the month. This page checks every day and shows the most recent month both have published.

Which hospital should I go to?

In an emergency, call 999 or go to your nearest emergency department. For anything less urgent, NHS 111 (or NHS 111 Wales online) can tell you where to go. These figures are monthly averages and do not tell you how busy either department is today.
