When to go to A&E

A&E is for serious injuries and illnesses that could be life-threatening, such as:

  • chest pain or severe difficulty breathing
  • signs of a stroke: face drooping, arm weakness or slurred speech (call 999)
  • heavy bleeding that will not stop
  • loss of consciousness, a fit that will not stop, or sudden confusion
  • a severe allergic reaction, serious burns or a serious head injury
  • someone at immediate risk after self-harm or an overdose
In a life-threatening emergency, call 999. Ambulance crews can start treatment on the way.

Living in Wales? What to know

You can use the Countess A&E wherever you live. Emergency departments treat anyone who needs emergency care.

For urgent problems that are not life-threatening, use NHS 111 England, either online at 111.nhs.uk or by phone. NHS 111 England can refer you to the hospital's urgent treatment centre and book you a time. NHS 111 Wales lists the Countess but says it cannot book a slot there for you.

If you call 111 from a phone in Flintshire you will normally reach NHS 111 Wales, which may direct you to Wrexham Maelor or another Welsh service instead. See our guide to Wrexham Maelor A&E.

The emergency department works alongside an urgent treatment centre on the same site, for problems that need same-day care but not A&E. You may be directed there after you arrive, or referred by NHS 111 England.

What happens when you arrive

You will be assessed first to check how urgent your problem is. The sickest patients are seen first, so you may wait longer than someone who arrived after you. You may be sent to the urgent treatment centre, a same-day emergency care service or a specialist team rather than the main emergency department.

If you can, bring a list of the medicines you take, a phone charger, and someone who can stay with you.

Getting there

The hospital is on Liverpool Road (the A5116), about three miles from Chester city centre on the north side of the city. Use CH2 1UL for sat nav.

Parking is charged, unlike at hospitals in Wales. Check the hospital's website for current prices and for help with costs if you are eligible.

By public transport: check bus times from Deeside with Traveline on 0871 200 22 33, or Traveline Cymru on 0800 464 0000.

Inspections

The Care Quality Commission (CQC) inspects NHS hospitals in England. After its most recent assessment of the hospital's urgent and emergency care, which began in January 2025, the CQC rated the service inadequate and issued the trust with a warning notice requiring significant improvements. The hospital as a whole is rated requires improvement.

The trust has since reported improvements in its emergency department, including more patients being seen within four hours.

Read the reports on the CQC website.

Questions

Will I have to pay as a Welsh resident?

No. NHS emergency care is free at the point of use for UK residents on both sides of the border. Parking at the hospital is charged.

Should I go to the Countess or the Maelor?

In an emergency, go to the nearest A&E or call 999 and the ambulance crew will decide. If it is not an emergency, use 111 first.

How do I raise a concern about care?

Speak to the nurse in charge at the time if you can. Afterwards, contact the hospital's Patient Advice and Liaison Service (PALS) through the trust's website.
