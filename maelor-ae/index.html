Wrexham Maelor A&E: a guide

When to go to A&E

A&E is for serious injuries and illnesses that could be life-threatening, such as:

  • chest pain or severe difficulty breathing
  • signs of a stroke: face drooping, arm weakness or slurred speech (call 999)
  • heavy bleeding that will not stop
  • loss of consciousness, a fit that will not stop, or sudden confusion
  • a severe allergic reaction, serious burns or a serious head injury
  • someone at immediate risk after self-harm or an overdose
In a life-threatening emergency, call 999. Ambulance crews can start treatment on the way.

If it is urgent but not an emergency

Call NHS 111 Wales on 111 or use 111.wales.nhs.uk. It is free and open 24 hours. It can send you to the right place, which might be an urgent primary care centre, a minor injuries unit, a pharmacy or an out-of-hours GP rather than A&E. For mental health support, call 111 and choose option 2.

Betsi Cadwaladr health board runs urgent primary care centres at Wrexham Maelor and at Mold Community Hospital. These see people with urgent problems on the same day, usually after a referral from 111 or your GP.

For minor illnesses, many community pharmacies in Flintshire can give advice and some treatment without an appointment.

What happens when you arrive

You will be triaged first: a nurse or clinician checks how urgent your problem is. The sickest patients are seen first, so you may wait longer than someone who arrived after you.

Some people are then directed to another service on site, such as the urgent primary care centre, a minor injuries service or a specialist team, rather than being seen by an emergency doctor.

If you can, bring a list of the medicines you take, a phone charger, and someone who can stay with you.

Getting there

The hospital is on Croesnewydd Road on the west side of Wrexham, signposted from the A483. From Deeside, most people take the A55 to its junction with the A483 near Chester, then the A483 south. Use LL13 7TD for sat nav.

The emergency department is signposted from the main hospital car park. If you are dropping someone off for A&E, follow the A&E signs rather than going to the main entrance.

Parking is free, as at all NHS hospitals in Wales, but the car parks are often full on weekdays. Allow extra time. Wrexham councillors called for an urgent review of parking at the hospital in September 2025.

By public transport: buses stop on Croesnewydd Road, and Wrexham General station is less than a mile away. Check times with Traveline Cymru on 0800 464 0000.

Inspections

Healthcare Inspectorate Wales (HIW) inspects NHS hospitals in Wales. Its most recent unannounced inspection of the emergency department was in December 2024, with the report published on 13 March 2025. HIW said progress had been made since its previous inspection but significant challenges remained, mainly high demand and delays in moving patients out of the department and through the hospital.

Read the reports on the HIW website.

Questions

I live in Deeside. Should I go to the Maelor or the Countess?

In an emergency, go to the nearest A&E or call 999 and the ambulance crew will decide. Both hospitals take patients from Flintshire. If it is not an emergency, call 111 first. See our guide to the Countess of Chester A&E for the English side of the border.

How do I raise a concern about care?

Speak to the nurse in charge at the time if you can. Afterwards, you can raise a concern with Betsi Cadwaladr health board under the NHS Wales Putting Things Right process, through the health board's website.
