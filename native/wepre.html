A 160-acre country park in Connah's Quay, run by Flintshire County Council, with ancient woodland, a waterfall, formal gardens, one of the region's best playgrounds and a Welsh castle in the woods. Here is what you need to know before you go.

What to see

Woodland and Wepre Brook. The heart of the park is a wooded valley of ancient sessile oak woodland, protected as part of the Connah's Quay Ponds and Woodlands Site of Special Scientific Interest. Wepre Brook runs through it.

The waterfall. The waterfall on Wepre Brook was built to power a turbine that made electricity for the Old Hall, the house that once stood here.

Old Hall and the gardens. The Old Hall was built in 1788 and the estate stayed in private hands until 1920. The hall has gone, but its cellars lie under today's visitor centre, and the formal gardens are planted much as they would have been in 1870.

Rosie Pool. A pool dug in the late 18th century to supply the estate with fish.

For children. The council describes the playground as the best free children's playground in the region. There is also a skatepark, and two football pitches.

Ewloe Castle

Hidden in the woods at the edge of the park is Ewloe Castle, a native Welsh castle. According to Cadw, which looks after it, the stone tower was probably built by Llywelyn ap Iorwerth, Llywelyn the Great, some time after 1210, and Llywelyn ap Gruffydd added the curtain walls and round western tower around 60 years later.

Entry is free and the castle is open all year in daylight hours. Dogs on short leads are allowed on the ground floor levels. From the park, a path follows Wepre Brook to the castle, on tracks with flights of steps.

Wepre parkrun

A free, timed 5km run every Saturday at 9am, open to everyone. The course stays inside the park: it starts by the river below the visitor centre and finishes on the grass beside it, passing the waterfall and Rosie Pool. It is hilly, with around 500 feet of climbing, on gravel, woodland paths and grass. Register before your first run at parkrun.org.uk/wepre.

Getting there

  • By car: to the visitor centre on Wepre Drive, Connah's Quay, CH5 4HL. Parking charges apply.
  • By train: Shotton station is about a mile away.
  • By bus: buses stop at Wepre Park Entrance and at Wepre War Memorial, a short walk away. Check times with Traveline Cymru.

Events and getting involved

Ranger-led events are listed by Flintshire County Council on its events page and the countryside service's Facebook page. The Friends of Wepre Park volunteer group works with the rangers and runs its own events: email friendsofweprepark@hotmail.co.uk or ask at the visitor centre.

The park is also home to the Connah's Quay Festival, run by Connah's Quay Town Council, which was held there in June 2026.

Questions

Is parking free?

No. Since 27 July 2026 it costs £1 for up to two hours, £2 for up to four hours or £4 for the day, between 9am and 9pm. Blue Badge holders in disabled bays do not pay.

Is Ewloe Castle in the park?

Yes, it sits in the woods at the edge of the park and is reached on foot along Wepre Brook. It is free to visit and open in daylight hours.

Is there a café and toilets?

Yes. The Old Hall Café is at the visitor centre, and there are toilets in the park.

Can I have a barbecue?

Please don't. North Wales Fire and Rescue Service has asked people not to light fires or use disposable barbecues after a fire was started in the park in July 2026.
